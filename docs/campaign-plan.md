# Omega Zero Day — campaign plan (draft 3; Acts I and II built)

Status: **story and plan only, nothing implemented.**
Direction agreed so far: **dark and grim** tone · **two non-human peoples** (native alien life *and* a lost proto-human colony) · the central twist is that **Helion, the player's own side, caused the collapse** · a **silent commander** the story speaks to.
Names are locked; the ending, Hollow and release decisions are in section 6.

---

## 1. The world

### Cinder and Helios
Cinder is a scorched world circling **Helios**, an old orange star. Thirty-one years ago the Helion Dynamics charter colony landed to mine **flux**: blue crystal that grows in vast fields and breathes out of deep **vents**. Flux powers everything, from jump drives to guns.

Officially, Helios is collapsing through bad luck: a rare instability, *nobody's fault*. In **214 days** it goes supernova. The **ark-fleet** in orbit can carry forty thousand sleepers out, but its drives are dry and the last flux is on the surface. The **launch corridor** is a narrow window through the star's dying magnetic shells, opened by a beacon fed from the vents. Only one fleet can ride it.

### The truth (revealed across the campaign)
**Flux is alive.** The crystal is the body of **the Vey**, a planet-wide mind that grows through Cinder's crust like a root system. The vents are where it breathes. For millions of years the Vey has done one thing: it **holds Helios steady**. Its resonance damps the star's magnetic storms, which is why an old, unstable star has a living world at all.

**Helion knew.** Nineteen years ago a company survey (the *Resonance Report*) found the link between harvesting and the star's flare activity. The board buried it, because flux was too profitable. Six years ago Helion went further: **Project Lantern**, a deep tap drilled into the Vey's heart to pull flux faster than any harvester could. Lantern worked. The Vey convulsed, its grip on Helios broke, and the countdown began.

**The player's callsign is Lantern.** Helion named its field commander after the project, either as a grim joke or as a test of how much the commander knew. The player learns why in Act II.

## 2. Peoples and factions

| | Who they are | What they want | How they fight |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Helion Dynamics** (player) | The charter company: miners, engineers, its security wing | Launch the fleet, and keep the truth buried with the planet | Balanced |
| **Kessler Collective** | The colony security corps, broken away under its marshal | To take the fleet *and* put Helion's board on trial in front of the sleepers | Armoured columns behind gun lines |
| **The Ashen Clans** | The first miners' grandchildren, left in the deep crust when Helion moved on. Two confederations: **the Hearth** (Reyes; they live beside the crystal and hold the old terraforming station) and **the Ashen** (Vessa; raiders and flux traders) | The Hearth: stop the harvest and let the Vey try to hold the sun. The Ashen: sell fleet seats, and keep cutting the Hollow's crystal-grown bodies for pure flux | Guerrillas. Old rifles, harpoons, firebombs, welded trucks and beast-drawn carts; hidden snares and mines; unpowered camps that pack up and move; raids that pay |
| **The Hollow** (new, mostly neutral) | Descendants of the colony ship **Meridian**, which landed 200 years ago and was listed as lost. The Vey grew into them: pale, partly crystalline, living in the deep tunnels | To be left alone. Then, as the Vey dies, to make someone pay | Ambushes from tunnels, crystal-grown infantry, units that heal near crystal |
| **The Vey** (not a faction) | The living crystal under everything | Unknown. It reacts to harvesting with tremors, crystal storms and vents that go dark | A force on the map, not an army |

### Characters

**Lantern (the player).** Helion's field commander. Never seen and never heard. Everyone speaks to Lantern, and the briefings never say whether Lantern knew.

**Admiral Ines Varga**, commanding the ark-fleet from orbit. She is calm, exhausted and counts sleepers the way other people count bullets. **She has read the Resonance Report.** She signed off on Project Lantern's fleet contract. Her briefings are honest about everything except that. *Arc:* from command, to evasion, to a confession she makes only once the corridor is open and nobody can turn back.

**Sergeant Mara Okoye**, Helion's ground lead. She has twenty years in colony security and served under Kessler before the split. She is blunt, funny under fire and loyal to the people beside her rather than to the company. *Arc:* she is the player's conscience. When the truth about Helion lands, she asks Lantern one question, "Did you know?", and never gets an answer. In Act III she either stays or walks to Kessler, depending on what Lantern does at the Meridian.

**Dr. Teodor Lind**, Helion's chief flux engineer and the architect of Project Lantern. He is warm, nervous and talks too much because silence is worse. He has spent six years trying to undo what he built. *Arc:* in Act II he gives Lantern the Resonance Report. In Act III he goes down the Lantern shaft to shut it off, and doesn't come back.

**Marshal Dieter Kessler**, leader of the Kessler Collective. He held the colony through two famines and found out about Lantern three years too late. He is the campaign's antagonist, and he is **right about Helion**. *Line:* "I don't want your fleet, Lantern. I want your board in a cell when it lands." *Arc:* the wall the player has to break, and the man who offers the only honest deal in the game.

**Speaker Ama Reyes**, a Helion biologist who walked out into the deep crust, was taken in by the Hearth clans and is now their elected voice. She is the one who first understood the Vey. She is gentle, stubborn and has nothing left to lose. *Arc:* she believes that if the harvesting stops, the Vey can hold Helios for decades. She is half right.

**Vessa Kol, "the Cinder Queen"**, chief of the Ashen clans. She is charming and mercenary, and she has been quietly harvesting Hollow captives for the purest flux on Cinder. *Arc:* never an ally, always a deal. Every deal she offers is paid for by the Hollow.

**Seren**, an elder of the Hollow, descended from the Meridian's pilot. She speaks through the crystal itself, in fragments and old colony slang two centuries out of date. *Arc:* she is first heard as an unidentified voice on the comms. She becomes a reluctant guide, and her people's fate is the campaign's last decision.

## 3. The story in three acts

### Act I — Landfall
Lantern drops into a burned-out Helion depot to restart flux production for the fleet. Kessler patrols probe the new base. Harvesters start disappearing near the deep fields, dragged into tunnels. Something is in the crust. Lind shows Lantern that the vents matter more than the crystal fields. In the act's last mission Kessler's first real assault comes with a broadcast to every channel: **"Ask your company what Project Lantern was."**

### Act II — The Corridor
Lind reveals the corridor, and every faction starts racing for the vents. Ashen raids on Helion's economy force a deal with Vessa Kol, paid in Hollow lives. Lantern breaks a Verdant hold on the central vents and finds their labs: the crystal is alive and in pain. Reyes asks Lantern to stop the harvest.

Then Lind hands over the Resonance Report. **Helion did this.** Okoye asks the question. Varga goes quiet on the comms for one full mission.

The act ends at the **wreck of the Meridian**, where the Hollow live. Ashen forces are cutting it apart for crystal. **The first choice:** protect the Hollow (the Ashen raid escalates and the next missions are harder) or let the Ashen take the wreck (Vessa pays well, and the Hollow turn hostile for the rest of the campaign).

### Act III — Last Light
Helios starts flaring and the surface burns. Kessler offers terms: a merged fleet under his command, with Helion's board arrested on landing, *including the commander*. Lantern refuses (there is no accept button; Lantern is Helion's) and has to break Kessler's wall. Lind goes down the Lantern shaft to shut the tap and dies doing it. His death buys the corridor its last window.

**Zero Day.** Every faction left converges on the corridor vents while the sun comes apart. Lantern has to hold them until the beacon fires.

### The ending: there is no good one
To fire, the beacon has to burn the vents dry, which means burning out what is left of the Vey. **The final choice**, made on the last mission's map:

- **Fire the beacon.** The fleet escapes. The Vey dies, Cinder dies, and with it the Hollow and the clans. Varga finally confesses on an open channel as the fleet jumps. Forty thousand sleepers wake up on the far side carrying the guilt.
- **Give the vents to Reyes.** The beacon never fires. The Vey, unbled, steadies Helios for a while: years, maybe decades, Reyes can't say. The fleet stays. Humanity lives on Cinder beside the Vey and the Hollow, under a sun that will still die, and the board's crimes are judged in the open.

- **Hidden third ending: carry the seed.** This is only possible if Lantern protected the Hollow at the Meridian. Seren reveals that the Vey can live in a single heart-crystal, and that it can be cut from the Lantern shaft and carried aboard the fleet. Doing it means holding the shaft *and* the corridor at once, and the Vey's absence lets Helios go nova at once instead of in hours. The fleet escapes with a living world-seed and the Hollow's survivors. Cinder, the Verdant Pact and everyone who couldn't reach the corridor in time die. It's the "best" ending, and it still costs a planet.

The Act II choice changes the details too: whether the Hollow stand with Lantern in the finale, whether Okoye is still at the commander's side, and what Seren's last line is.

## 4. Missions

| # | Mission | Enemy | Teaches / uses | Win | Story beat |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Cold Drop** | Kessler patrols | Building, harvesting, power | Build an Extractor, deliver 3,000 flux, clear the patrol camp | Okoye and Varga introduced. A harvester goes missing at the end |
| 2 | **Flux Lines** | Hearth Clan pickets and snares, **Hollow ambush** | Capturing vents and towers, vision | Hold 2 vents and a tower for 60 s | Lind introduced. First tunnel ambush; an unknown voice on the comms (Seren) |
| 3 | **Iron Weather** | Kessler | Defences, repair, upgrades | Survive 3 scripted waves, then destroy the forward base | Kessler's broadcast: *ask about Project Lantern* |
| 4 | **Sparks** | Ashen | Protecting the economy, raids | Keep 3 harvesters alive for 8 min while building a Fabricator | Vessa's first deal: the raids stop for 2 min, and she says nothing about where her flux comes from |
| 5 | **The Green Tide** | Hearth Clans | Infantry counters, sweeping for traps | Break the Hearth's hold on the central vents | Reyes's labs: the crystal is alive. The vents scream on capture (the Vey reacts) |
| 6 | **Resonance** | Kessler and Hollow | Research and orbital strike under a timer | Destroy Kessler's Orbital Uplink before it charges | Lind hands over the Resonance Report. Okoye asks the question |
| 7 | **Meridian** | Ashen, Hollow | Escort and defence, **choice** | Defend the wreck *or* let the Ashen take it | First choice. Seren speaks clearly for the first time |
| 8 | **Kessler's Wall** | Kessler | Siege: bastions, walls, colossus | Destroy Kessler's Nexus | Kessler's terms. Lind goes down the Lantern shaft (a side objective: hold the shaft entrance) |
| 9 | **Zero Day** | Everyone left | Everything | Hold the corridor for 5 min with the supernova running, then **choose** | Final choice. With the Meridian flag, a third option appears: also hold the Lantern shaft to cut the seed |

There are 9 missions of 12–25 minutes each, about 3 hours in all.

### New mechanics the story needs
- **The Hollow as a neutral-hostile faction.** They come out of tunnel mouths near deep crystal, with 3 new units (crystal-grown infantry, a burrower that surfaces under harvesters, and a "choir" support unit that heals Hollow near crystal). Their stance (hostile, neutral or allied) is set per mission and by the Act II choice.
- **Vey resonance.** Over-harvesting a field raises a local resonance meter. High resonance sets off tremors (damage to structures), crystal storms (vision loss) and vents going dark. This is the campaign's proof that the crystal is alive, and it could later become an optional skirmish rule.
- **Choices.** Two map-based decisions (missions 7 and 9) set campaign flags that change later missions and the ending. The third ending in mission 9 only appears if the mission 7 flag is set.

## 5. Implementation plan

Nothing starts until this draft is signed off. The plan keeps the current file ownership.

### Phase 0 — engine groundwork (Claude: `game.ts`, `ai.ts`, `map.ts`)
1. **Objectives replace the hard-coded win check.** An objective list is evaluated every tick, and skirmish becomes the default list, so it plays the same as now.
   - Types: `destroyAll`, `destroyBuilding`, `holdCorridor`, `captureN`, `survive`, `deliverFlux`, `protectUnits`, `holdArea`, `timer`, `choice`.
   - Each objective is primary or optional, and can stay hidden until a trigger reveals it.
2. **Event bus.** `unitDied`, `buildingDestroyed`, `captured`, `corridorChanged`, `enteredArea`, `timeReached`, `objectiveDone`, `resonanceChanged`.
3. **Scripted actions.** Spawn a wave, open a tunnel, reveal an area, give credits, unlock tech, set an AI posture or a faction stance, set a flag, end the mission.
4. **Hand-authored maps.** A fixed seed plus overrides for bases, vents, towers, crystal, tunnel mouths and the Meridian wreck.
5. **Tech locks and starting tech** per mission.
6. **Hollow faction:** 3 units, tunnel spawning, stance rules, and an AI that defends its territory instead of building.
7. **Vey resonance:** per-field meters fed by harvesting, with tremor, storm and dark-vent effects.

### Phase 1 — mission data and the first slice
- `src/campaign/missions.ts` holds pure data (map, factions, start state, objectives, triggers, dialogue). `src/campaign/runner.ts` loads a mission and wires its triggers and flags.
- Build **missions 1–3** as a vertical slice and playtest them with you before Acts II and III.

### UI agent's part (`index.html`, `ui.ts`, `main.ts`)
- A Campaign / Skirmish choice and a mission select.
- Mission briefing cards (Varga's transmissions).
- A **comms box**: portrait, name and typed-out line; skippable and never blocking. It also needs a "distorted" style for Seren speaking through the crystal.
- An objective tracker, a **choice prompt**, mission complete and failed screens, and the ending sequences.
- Campaign progress in localStorage.

### Claude's art part (`art.ts`, `render.ts`)
- Portraits for the seven characters, code-drawn in the refined style.
- The Hollow: 3 new units and tunnel mouths.
- The Meridian wreck, the Lantern shaft, the launch-beacon tower, and resonance effects (crystal pulses, tremor dust, dark vents).

### Order of work
**First release = Act I plus a cliffhanger:** missions 1–3, ending on Kessler's broadcast, *"Ask your company what Project Lantern was."*
1. Phase 0 engine work, but only the parts Act I needs: objectives, the event bus, scripted waves and spawns, hand-authored maps, tech locks, and a basic **tunnel ambush** for the Hollow's first appearance in mission 2 (no full Hollow faction yet). Then re-test skirmish.
2. Missions 1–3, the comms box, the objective tracker, briefing cards and a "to be continued" card. Playtest it with you.
3. Acts II and III after that: the full Hollow faction, Vey resonance, missions 4–9, the choices and all three endings.
4. **The Hollow in skirmish** come later, as an optional neutral faction in the skirmish settings once they're balanced in the campaign.
5. A balance pass using the telemetry.

## 6. Built so far
- **Act I** (I-1 Cold Drop, I-2 Flux Lines, I-3 Iron Weather) and **Act II** (II-4 Sparks, II-5 The Green Tide, II-6 Resonance).
- Act II added: the Hollow as a faction (Shardkin, Burrower, Choir; tunnel mouths), Vey resonance (tremors, crystal storms, dark vents), comms-box choices (Vessa's deal), the shielded Uplink and its relays.
- **The Keepers** (the Vey's makers) arrive in II-6 *Resonance*, making it a three-way fight with Kessler; also a skirmish option.
- Changes from the plan: II-6 is *Resonance* (the Uplink mission, with the Report and Okoye's question), so the Meridian choice moves to Act III as planned (mission 7).
- **Act III** (III-7 Meridian, III-8 Kessler's Wall, III-9 Zero Day). Campaign flags carry choices forward: `labs` (II-5, spared or burned), `meridian` (protected or sold), `okoye` (stayed or left), `lind` (gone). Sparing the labs brings Verdant in as allies at Zero Day; protecting the Meridian brings the Hollow as allies and unlocks the third ending.
- **Three human factions** (the user's call): Helion and Kessler stay futuristic (Kessler gets riveted armour, rust stripes and war banners over Helion's shapes); the Verdant Pact is gone, and **the Ashen Clans** are an older, scavenging people with their own units, buildings and art (`src/clan-art.ts`). In the campaign, Reyes's **Hearth Clans** (green, slot 2) take the Pact's role and Vessa's **Ashen Clans** (slot 3) are the raiders.
- Balance pass with the fair-play bot against the old build: II-4 and II-5 match; the Meridian defence was too hard before and is now eased (ship 7000 hp, 7:00 timer, smaller waves, mortars late). Zero Day had a bug where allied vents didn't count toward the beacon (and allies blocked each other's captures); fixed, and Vessa's raids now press the vents so the no-ally route isn't a walkover.
- Act III play-test with a player-like script (forward defence, mortar hunting, focus fire) as well as the passive bot:
  - **III-7 Meridian:** the Hollow now hold their fire until you choose (an army moving up before the prompt used to shoot the ship down). Waves every 45 s, growing; mortars from 2:30. Good play wins cleanly, ignoring the mortars loses.
  - **III-8 Kessler's Wall:** Kessler sends armour at the shaft every 30 s while Lind is down; the shaft has 6000 hp, Kessler's Nexus is hardened (level 3) and he starts with 10,000. Good play wins with real losses; a passive player holds the shaft but can't take the wall.
  - **III-9 Zero Day:** the hardest route (no allies) is a close win with good play. Hollow ambushes go for the vents, not the base, and losing the base has its own ending text.
- The Mortar Cart was too strong against packed infantry (one cart wiped 45 units): damage 70 → 55, splash 1.4 → 1.1.
- The Clans have their own sounds: powder rifles, a rattling machine gun, a harpoon spring-gun, firebombs that smash and catch, black-powder cannons, a mortar's cough, a snare's snap.
- How the Clans fight is explained in play: Okoye's notes in the I-2 briefing, a line in II-4's, one-time field notes the first time a hidden trap springs on you or a raider earns flux off you, and three tips at the start of a skirmish as the Clans.
- **Campaign difficulty** (Easy / Normal / Hard, under the mission list, kept with your progress): every scripted enemy group thins on Easy (every third unit stays home) and thickens on Hard (every third brings a friend); starting flux ×1.3 / ×0.85; the rival AI runs at that difficulty.
- **Bigger alien rosters** (the Brood and the Keepers felt thin to play): the Brood add the Lurker (anti-armour spines), the Brood Nurse (heals) and the Stalker (fast pouncer), plus an Evolution Pit for research; the Keepers add the Seraph (shielded blades), the Oracle (long-range sniper), the Herald (anti-air flyer) and the Arbiter (light artillery), plus an Archive for research. Their AIs use the new units too.
- **The Hollow are a skirmish faction**, to play or to face: Hollow Hearth, Tunnel Mouths, Geodes (income, more beside crystal), Grotto (research), Echo Spires; Shardkin, Burrower, Choir, Delver and Cairn. **Burrow**: Shardkin, Burrowers and Delvers go into the rock and surface anywhere you have scouted (60 s cooldown). As an enemy they grow on the crystal fields and send raids that half burrow up beside the target.
- **Skirmish landscapes**: Scrub plains, Ash flats (basalt spires, scattered crystal, no lakes), Crystal canyons (red rock ridges with passes), Dead city (a ruined colony street grid), or random. The campaign stays on its hand-made plains.
- **Epilogue and credits** after Zero Day: an epilogue for the ending you reached, shaded by your choices (the labs, the Meridian, Okoye), then a credits roll; every mission stays open to replay with other choices.
- **Balance pass on every faction** with equal-cost army fights against three Helion armies (balanced, anti-infantry, armoured), sides swapped to cancel terrain: alien and Clan unit costs raised until each side lands near Helion's own score; the Monolith now needs an Archive, the Acolyte's rifle no longer does sniper damage, the Seraph's shield is smaller, Shrine and Geode income are lower, and the player Brood start with five units, not eight. Fixed: a big crowd could shove units inside a building's footprint, where they could never path out.
- **Unit voices**: a short synthesised bark when you select your units or give an order. Helion and Kessler over radio (Kessler lower and gruffer), the Clans shout, the brood chitters, the Keepers hum, the Hollow chime.
- **Boot camp**: an optional first mission before Act I (and the default for a new player) where Okoye walks through moving, power, an Extractor, flux, a Barracks, a vent and an attack, one step at a time.
- **Richer art for the newer factions**: the Hollow drawn in faceted stone and crystal clusters with scraps of the Meridian (riveted hull plates, stencils, cable and lamps); the Lurker, Brood Nurse and Stalker segmented and veined; the Keepers (Acolyte, Seraph, Oracle) in layered robes with gold pauldrons and filigree, a halo and hex-light shield on the Seraph, a hooded Oracle with an orbiting eye-crystal, a panelled Herald and a segmented lens array for the Arbiter; a stepped Archive and a churning Evolution Pit; brighter, more distinct Clan infantry (a Scav's hat and goggles, a harpoon's flukes, a firebrand's bottles and rags) and lived-in Clan buildings (woodpiles, crates, barrels, lanterns, hides on racks, smoke and sparks).
- **The Hollow, upgraded (faction art pass 1 of 5):** a 3D creature rig in `hollow-art.ts` (tapered limbs, shaded masses, draped cloth, crystal growths, glows, faceted stones, all placed in 3D and drawn back to front for the heading, with walk cycles). The Shardkin, Burrower and Choir are creatures now, not reskinned soldiers; the Delver and Cairn walk on the same rig. Hollow units climb up out of the ground (from Tunnel Mouths, when burrowing, and in the campaign's tunnel ambushes).
- New portraits for Reyes (a Hearth settlement in the deep rock, crystal greenhouse) and Vessa (a raider chief in scrap armour by a burning drum).

## 7. Decisions
- **Names:** locked. Cinder, Lantern, the Vey, the Hollow, Meridian.
- **Ending:** two bleak endings, plus a hidden third (carry the seed) unlocked by protecting the Hollow in mission 7.
- **The Hollow:** campaign first, an optional skirmish faction later.
- **First release:** Act I (missions 1–3) ending on a cliffhanger.
